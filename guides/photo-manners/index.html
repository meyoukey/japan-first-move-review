What to Do First Before Taking Photos in Japan

Photos are usually fine in many places, but some spots have rules.

Before you take a photo, check for signs or ask if you are unsure.

First Move

Look for signs first, then ask if photos are okay.

Quick steps

  1. Look for no-photo or no-flash signs.
  2. Check before taking photos in shrines, temples, shops, or exhibitions.
  3. If people are close to your camera, ask first or avoid taking the photo.
  4. If staff asks you to stop, lower your camera right away.

Don’t Do This

Don’t ignore no-photo or no-flash signs.

Some places allow photos without flash. Others do not allow photos at all.

Don’t take close-up photos of people without permission.

Be careful around people praying, staff, customers, children, or anyone close to your camera.

Don’t block paths or entrances for a photo.

Move to the side, especially in crowded places or near shrine and temple approaches.

Say or show this

Use these phrases to speak or point when you need help.

Use before taking photos in a shop, shrine, temple, or exhibit. Shashin o totte mo daijoubu desu ka? 写真を撮っても大丈夫ですか？ Can I take photos?
Use when you want to photograph a specific item, display, or area. Kore o totte mo daijoubu desu ka? これを撮っても大丈夫ですか？ Can I take a photo of this?
Use if staff or someone nearby asks you to stop. Sumimasen, yamemasu. すみません、やめます。 Sorry, I will stop.

If You See or Hear This

Satsuei kinshi 撮影禁止 It means: No photography. Your move Put your camera away.
Furasshu kinshi フラッシュ禁止 It means: No flash photography. Your move Turn off the flash, or skip the photo if you are unsure.
Satsuei wa goenryo kudasai. 撮影はご遠慮ください It means: Please refrain from taking photos. Your move Do not take photos here.

If You’re Stuck

If you are not sure, ask staff or skip the photo. If someone corrects you, stop right away and lower your camera.

Tiny Culture Note

Photo rules can change by room, object, or ceremony. A nearby sign matters more than what other people are doing.
