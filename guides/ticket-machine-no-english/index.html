What to Do When a Ticket Machine Has No English

If you are standing in front of a ticket machine in Japan and there is no English button, don’t start pressing random buttons.

Ticket machines can appear at ramen shops, train stations, buses, museums, sento, and other everyday places.

First Move

Look for an English button first, then use camera translation if there isn’t one.

Quick steps

  1. Check the corners of the screen for English or language settings.
  2. If there is no English, use camera translation.
  3. Look for the simplest option that matches what you need.
  4. Ask staff before pressing random buttons.
  5. Keep the ticket or receipt if you make a mistake.
  6. After paying, check if there is a change button. Some machines do not return coins until you press it.

Don’t Do This

Don’t press random buttons just to move forward.

Some machines are hard to cancel after payment, and staff may need the ticket or receipt to help you.

Don’t block the machine while translating for too long.

If people are waiting behind you, step aside for a moment and let someone else go first.

Don’t throw away the ticket or receipt.

Even if you bought the wrong thing, it may help staff understand what happened.

Say or show this

Use these phrases to speak or point when you need help.

Use when you want to ask if the machine can show English. Eigo ni dekimasu ka? 英語にできますか？ Can I switch this to English?
Use when you don’t know how to use it. Tsukaikata ga wakarimasen. 使い方がわかりません。 I don’t know how to use this.
Use when you can point to what you want to buy. Kore o kaitai desu. これを買いたいです。 I want to buy this.

If You See or Hear This

English / Language / Gengo English / Language / 言語 It means: Language setting. Your move Tap it first. If you cannot find a language option, use camera translation or ask staff.
Kounyuu 購入 It means: Purchase or buy. Your move Press it when you have selected what you want and are ready to pay.
Otsuri / Henkyaku おつり / 返却 It means: Change / return. Your move Press it after buying if your change does not come out automatically.
Torikeshi 取消 It means: Cancel. Your move Press it only if you want to cancel or go back.
Kakunin 確認 It means: Confirm. Your move Press it when you are ready to confirm your choice.

If You’re Stuck

Step aside and reset. Take a photo of the machine, translate it, and decide before you touch the screen again. If there is a staff member nearby, show your phone with a simple sentence.

Tiny Culture Note

Many Japanese ticket machines are built for speed, not for first-time visitors. Stepping aside is often the smoothest move.
