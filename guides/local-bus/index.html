How to Ride a Local Bus in Japan

If a local bus arrives in Japan and you don’t know whether to enter from the front or the back, pause for one second.

The system can change depending on the city or bus company. Your best first move is to watch how other passengers board.

First Move

Watch where other passengers board, then use the same door.

Quick steps

  1. Check which door other passengers use.
  2. If you board from the back, take a numbered ticket if there is one.
  3. Press the stop button before your stop.
  4. Check the fare display near the driver.
  5. Prepare coins or an IC card before you pay.
  6. Pay when you get off, unless the local system is pay-first.

Don’t Do This

Don’t assume every bus works the same way.

Some buses are pay-first. Some are pay-when-you-get-off. Some use a flat fare. Others use numbered tickets and fare zones.

Don’t wait until the last second to press the stop button.

Press it after your stop is announced or appears on the screen.

Say or show this

Use these phrases to speak or point when you need help.

Use when you want to confirm the destination. Replace Kyoto Station with your destination or stop. Kono basu wa Kyoto-eki ni ikimasu ka? このバスは京都駅に行きますか？ Does this bus go to Kyoto Station?
Use when you want to ask if IC cards work. IC kaado wa tsukaemasu ka? ICカードは使えますか？ Can I use an IC card?
Use when you want to ask if you pay when getting off. Oriru toki ni haraimasu ka? 降りる時に払いますか？ Do I pay when I get off?

If You See or Hear This

Seiriken o totte kudasai. 整理券を取ってください。 It means: Please take a numbered ticket. Your move Take the small ticket when you board.
Ato-barai desu. 後払いです。 It means: Pay when you get off. Your move Keep your ticket and pay near the driver when leaving.
Mae kara notte kudasai. 前から乗ってください。 It means: Please board from the front. Your move Use the front door.

If You’re Stuck

Watch what boarding passengers do. If people tap an IC card when boarding, do the same. If they take a numbered ticket, take one too.

Tiny Culture Note

Local buses in Japan are practical, not always beginner-friendly. Even Japanese travelers can feel unsure in a new city.
