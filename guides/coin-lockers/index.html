How to Use Coin Lockers in Japan

If you find coin lockers in Japan but don’t know how the system works, start with the locker itself.

The hardest part is often finding the same locker area later.

First Move

Before you walk away, take a photo of the locker number, nearby signs, and the closest exit or landmark.

Quick steps

  1. Find an empty locker that fits your bag.
  2. Put your luggage inside before paying.
  3. Check whether it uses a key, coins, IC card, or touch screen.
  4. Pay, lock the door, and keep the key, receipt, QR code, or IC card safe.
  5. If you use an IC card, remember that the same card may be needed to reopen the locker.
  6. Take photos of the locker number, locker area, and nearby signs.

Don’t Do This

Don’t walk away without checking how to reopen the locker.

Some lockers use a physical key. Some use a receipt or QR code. Some use the same IC card you paid with.

Don’t trust your memory in a large station or big facility.

Large stations and shopping complexes can have many locker areas that look similar.

Say or show this

Use these phrases to speak or point when you need help.

Use when you want to ask if lockers are available. Aiteiru rokkaa wa arimasu ka? 空いているロッカーはありますか？ Are there any available lockers?
Use when you can’t find the locker area again. Rokkaa no basho ga wakarimasen. ロッカーの場所がわかりません。 I can’t find the locker area.
Use when you can show a photo or receipt. Kono rokkaa wa doko ni arimasu ka? このロッカーはどこにありますか？ Where is this locker?

If You See or Hear This

Aki 空き It means: Available. Your move You can choose this locker if it fits your bag.
Shiyouchuu 使用中 It means: In use. Your move Look for another locker.
IC kaado senyou ICカード専用 It means: IC card only. Your move Use an IC card to pay. Keep the same card because it may be needed to reopen the locker.

If You’re Stuck

Search your photos first. Look for the locker number, nearby signs, exit name, shop sign, or floor map in the background. If you still cannot find it, show your locker key, receipt, or photo to staff nearby.

Tiny Culture Note

The hard part is not always locking your bag. It is finding the same locker area again later.
